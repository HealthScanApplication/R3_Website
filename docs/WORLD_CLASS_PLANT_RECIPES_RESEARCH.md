# World-class plant-forward recipe research

Research date: 2026-10-23

## Goal and method

This collection is a curated intersection of taste, nutritional usefulness, cultural breadth, and a recipe that can be cooked reliably at home. It is not presented as an objective world ranking.

- **Taste signal (40%)**: current audience ratings and traditional-dish coverage from TasteAtlas. TasteAtlas itself cautions that its rankings are not a final global conclusion.
- **Nutrition (40%)**: preference for legumes, vegetables, whole grains, nuts/seeds, and useful protein/fibre per serving. Nutrition estimates use USDA FoodData Central reference foods and the gram weights in each recipe.
- **Cookability (10%)**: available ingredients, clear steps, and a repeatable home-kitchen result.
- **Breadth (10%)**: distinct cuisines, textures, meal slots, and flavour profiles.

The values stored in `nutrition_per_serving` are planning estimates, not laboratory analyses. Brand choice, cooking yield, salt, and absorbed frying oil can materially change them.

## Nutrition design decisions

- Legume dishes pair protein with substantial dietary fibre. Harvard's Nutrition Source recommends legumes, soy, nuts, seeds, and whole grains as useful plant-protein sources.
- Lentil and bean dishes include tomato, pepper, or lemon because vitamin C helps absorption of non-haem iron.
- The two vegetarian dishes add protein and calcium but are labelled for egg or milk allergens and score slightly lower where saturated fat or sodium is higher.
- Ta'ameya includes baked/air-fried instructions as the default; authentic shallow-frying remains an option, with the nutrition estimate based on limited oil absorption.
- The collection does not imply that any one recipe supplies vitamin B12 for a vegan diet. People eating fully vegan diets need reliable fortified foods or supplementation.

## Image direction

Every hero image uses the same visual system: a real hand holding the finished bowl or plate, close editorial food photography, shallow depth of field (roughly an 85 mm lens at f/1.8), soft window light, natural food texture, culturally appropriate serving ware, no text, no logos, and no synthetic-looking perfection.

## Cookbook-library curation

The linked Drive library contained 100 PDFs representing 50 unique titles; half were duplicate copies. Recipe concepts were reviewed from the strongest culinary sources, including *Vegan Soul Kitchen*, *The Indian Vegan Kitchen*, *reFresh*, *Vegan Soups and Hearty Stews for All Seasons*, and *Get It Ripe*. Ten first-pass recipes were selected for flavour, cultural breadth and catalog gaps: Citrus Collards, Gumbo Z, Root-Vegetable Ital Stew, Garlicky Lima-Bean Spread, Baingan Bharta, Palak Chana Dal, Kale-Tofu Pulao, Matar Tofu Curry, Coconut Tempeh, and Marrakesh Stew.

These are independently written ROUTINE³ formulations, not copied cookbook recipes. Detox, cleanse, alkalising, aphrodisiac, immune-treatment, liver-treatment and disease-treatment claims are discarded. Books carrying those claims may contribute only ordinary culinary ideas that can be independently formulated and nutritionally modeled.

For this set, recipe-level nutrition numbers are not manually copied or estimated. Every gram-weighted ingredient links through `catalog_recipe_ingredients` to `catalog_ingredients`; macros derive from `nutrition_per_100g`, and micronutrients derive through `catalog_ingredient_elements`. `catalog_recipe_completeness` exposes missing links or open fields for QA.
